# La Paz Batchoy Artifact Gallery

## Artifact title

**The Bowl of La Paz Batchoy**

## Lore story

Born in the district of La Paz in Iloilo City, La Paz Batchoy is more than a
noodle soup—it is a living record of the people who cooked, sold, and shared it
across generations. Its history is closely tied to the public market and to the
meeting of Hokkien Chinese culinary influence with Ilonggo ingredients and
ingenuity. Even its name is believed to have Chinese roots connected with
“meat soup,” although the dish’s exact beginnings are remembered through
several competing family and community stories.

At the heart of the bowl are fresh miki noodles and a deeply flavored broth,
traditionally enriched with pork, offal, and sometimes beef. Ginamos adds a
distinctive savory depth, while garlic, scallions, and crushed chicharon finish
the dish. Each batchoyan guards its own method, but every version carries the
warmth of the market, the comfort of a shared meal, and the identity of Iloilo.

The recovered bowl in *Sabor sang Panahon* represents that living heritage.
Its ingredients are fragments of memory: knowledge passed from vendor to
apprentice, from grandparent to grandchild, and from one generation of
Ilonggos to the next. Finding it does not simply restore a forgotten recipe—it
restores the stories, labor, and sense of home held within every serving of La
Paz Batchoy.

## Ambient audio

**Title:** *Higop sang Handumanan* (*A Sip of Memory*)

An original instrumental gallery theme created for the moment the missing
Batchoy Bowl is recovered. Its ceramic-like opening chime represents the
artifact's discovery, while gentle plucked strings, a flute-like melody, and
soft percussion move from nostalgia toward a warm sense of cultural renewal.

## Historical note

The dish's exact inventor and earliest form remain subjects of multiple
community accounts. This gallery text therefore attributes La Paz Batchoy to
the district and its culinary community rather than presenting one disputed
origin story as settled fact.
